# NorthStar Retail — AI Returns & Refund Operations Platform

## Phase 2 — Business Problem

### Current-State Problem

NorthStar Retail's standard in-policy returns can usually be processed through existing return workflows. The larger operational problem occurs when a return or refund becomes an exception.

For exception cases, employees may need to investigate information across multiple enterprise systems, including POS, OMS, payments, shipping and fulfillment, customer and loyalty, inventory, returns, and ITSM systems.

Employees must manually reconstruct the case, determine which information is authoritative, identify the applicable return or refund policy, resolve conflicting system information, determine whether an action is permitted, and identify the appropriate team or approver when escalation is required.

This fragmented investigation process can increase resolution time, create inconsistent handling, require repeated customer contacts, increase operational effort, and make it difficult to reconstruct why a particular decision was made.

### Business Impact

The exception-handling process affects both operational efficiency and customer experience.

Potential business impacts include:

- Longer return and refund exception resolution times
- Increased employee time spent searching across systems
- Repeated customer contacts for unresolved cases
- Unnecessary escalations to managers and specialist teams
- Higher operational cost per exception case
- Greater risk of inconsistent return and refund decisions
- Refund delays caused by investigation or payment-processing issues
- Reduced customer satisfaction when case status or reasoning is unclear
- Increased effort required for internal reviews and customer disputes
- Limited auditability when evidence, recommendations, approvals, and actions are distributed across multiple systems

The business problem is therefore not simply that employees use many systems. The larger issue is the operational cost, delay, inconsistency, and customer impact created when employees must manually coordinate information and decisions across those systems during exception handling.
